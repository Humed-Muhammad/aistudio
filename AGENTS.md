# Humed’s AI Studio

A local video studio for short-form creators and the people who edit for them. The tools share one server (`make run`, http://127.0.0.1:8770) and one sidebar: the **Shorts editor** (`/shorts`), **YouTube to Shorts**, the **YouTube channel** tool (`/shorts?view=channel`), **Motion graphics** (`/graphics`) and the **Audio cleaner** (`/cleaner`). Everything renders on this Mac; cloud calls (Claude API, ElevenLabs, fal.ai, YouTube) happen only with the user's own keys and are said out loud before they cost money.

For installation or onboarding, run the installer for the user's system (`bash install-mac.sh`, `bash install-ubuntu.sh`, or `install-windows.ps1`, which runs the Ubuntu installer inside WSL2), as described under **Install** in `README.md`; developers can use `make setup`, `make shorts`, `make graphics`. Then check with `python doctor.py --all`. Do not spend paid API credit during setup. A running UI is not the same as a ready tool: check `doctor.py`.

General rules:
- Never change the creator's original upload; never regenerate or alter their voice.
- Ask before paid or cloud steps the user has not already approved (Claude API runs, ElevenLabs Strong noise removal or Scribe) and before large downloads.
- Report failures honestly with the real error; never claim a render or result exists without the file.
- Never print or commit keys. `.env`, `clients.json`, `channels.json`, `youtube-oauth.json`, `youtube-token.json`, `runs/`, `models/` and `sfx-library/` stay out of git.
- The plain-HTML tools (`static/graphics.html`, `static/cleaner.html`) share `static/tools.css` and `static/tools.js` (sidebar + theme). The sidebar and theme tokens mirror `motion/studio/src/styles.css` and `motion/studio/src/theme.ts`: change them together.


## Shorts editor (9:16 motion graphics)

A separate local workflow at `/shorts` (UI) or `shorts.py` (CLI). It adds captions, text pops, zoom punch-ins, shakes, flashes, sound effects, text behind the subject and images or emoji that follow a hand or the face. It makes **no generation calls**. Speech is only cut, never regenerated. When the whole recording is kept and noise removal is off, its original audio packets are copied unchanged. Sound effects are mixed on top. Install once with `make shorts`. Check with `doctor.py --shorts`.

1. `python shorts.py prepare VIDEO [--preset bold|boxed|minimal|none|auto]` transcribes the full recording and splits it into numbered parts. Several recordings uploaded together are kept in `parts/` and joined in natural file-name order into `upload.mp4` first (`shorts.join_recordings`; 30 fps, audio re-encoded once as AAC 256k and padded to each part's video length). With `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` set it uses ElevenLabs Scribe (cloud, paid per minute, required for Amharic; the audio is sent to ElevenLabs), otherwise local whisper.cpp, which cannot transcribe Amharic. Each job has a spoken language (`language` in `workflow-mode.json`: am, en or auto; default am). Do not use auto for Amharic: Scribe can detect English and silently omit all Amharic words. A long gap between words is only removed as a pause when the audio is quiet, and `transcript-check.json` reports speech with no transcript; report it and re-transcribe instead of cutting. Steps can be cancelled (`POST /shorts/jobs/ID/cancel`). Recordings mix Amharic and English: never drop speech because of its language, and write on-screen text in the spoken language (Ethiopic script for Amharic).
2. Cut to what matters before anything else. `python shorts.py cut JOB` prints the parts. There is no target length: drop retakes (keep the best, usually the last complete take), false starts, repeated points, tangents and dead air; keep every distinct point, needed context and the payoff. When the user asks Claude Code to cut, write `{"keep":[ids in order],"removed":[{"id":3,"why":"retake"}],"summary":"…"}` and save it with `--file`. `--auto` calls the Claude API with the transcript text. `tighten` (default on) also removes filler words and pauses over `max_pause`; a held hesitation the transcript left out ("ehh", "mmm", እህ: voiced throughout with an unchanging sound, `shorts.held_sounds`) counts as a pause, not missed speech. `--apply` builds `source.mp4`, cutting the original audio only at those points with short fades, then runs local MediaPipe hand, gesture, face and person-cutout tracking. Tracking is set per video (`tracking` in `workflow-mode.json`, `track_mode` in the API, Upload and Cut tabs): `full` (default; face, hands and cutout, about 5× the video length), `fast` (face and hands, no cutout, about 1×: no studio looks or text behind the subject) or `off` (only shots and keyframes, for screen recordings; uploads may run to `SHORTS_MAX_SECONDS_OFF`, 60 min, instead of `SHORTS_MAX_SECONDS`, 10 min; a wide recording is placed whole across the middle of a 1080×1920 frame over a blurred copy (`shorts.fit_frame`): captions sit at `caption_y`, no reframe, hand stickers or gesture triggers). It applies at the next rebuild. Review the cut with sound and `tracking-preview.mp4`. Hand `left`/`right` means the side of the screen.
3. Follow `docs/shorts-style.md` (look, card templates, sound rules); the auto-planner receives it too. `sweep` (light sweep: the phrase sits dim and a light reveals each letter as it is spoken, with a smooth whole-line lift) and the `wide` font (Montserrat Latin + Noto Ethiopic) follow the creator's Rofi Edits reference; `caption_motion` `lift` gives duo a smooth wave: each word rises as spoken, its letters following one after another. `caption_speed` (0.6-2, default 1, Captions tab) lengthens or shortens caption animations; planning keeps it. Captions default to `duo` (two-line glass, Noto Sans Ethiopic Black, line 2 in the accent; no black outlines, no yellow unless the creator asks: then `gold` or `cream` from the palette). Captions sit just under the speaker's chin by default (`caption_place` face: follows the face per phrase, never over the mouth, inside the safe zone 14–80% of the height clear of platform buttons); `fixed` uses `caption_y`. The creator picks style, font (`caption_font`), letters (`caption_fill`), motion, colour and position on the Captions tab with a live preview; once they do (`captions_auto` false) the planner keeps them. Fill `emphasis` for editorial and rise. Cards and text pops follow one **graphic style** per video (`graphics_style`: `glass` liquid glass, `bold` marker blocks, `editorial` serif accents, `minimal` no panels, `tech` dark grid + glow, `brutal` thick outline + hard shadow, `aurora` violet-coral gradient, `candy` pastel round, `noir` black + serif, `swiss` square caps, `clay` soft 3D, `cartoon` flat cream sticker cards with an ink outline and rounded Fredoka type; `motion/src/graphicKits.ts` sets fonts, type scale, surfaces, corners, accent treatment and scene backgrounds). The planner picks it unless the creator chose one (`graphics_auto` false, Graphics tab). Card headlines must not repeat the step or list text. Each card can take its own `accent` and `ink` (text) colour and an overlay card can sit `behind_subject`; text pops take `ink` and `color` (starred words); empty uses the style and video accent. The Graphics tab edits any event in place with a live preview. `card` events (`template` steps|slider|stat|icons|list|chips|quote|title, `scene` overlay|dim|dark|light|split, `text` with `*accent*` words, `items`, `value`, `label`, `active`) carry structure; `icons` items are `icon-name label` (names from `motion/src/film/icons.tsx`, Lucide), a leading `-` crosses one out and `+` ticks it, and a `stat` draws its chart only for growth figures (+240%, 3x). Graphics never cover the face: the planner and chat get the head and caption position (`shorts.face_note`), and `check_plan` moves any text pop, overlay or dim card, screen image or library graphic that would cover it to the nearest free spot under the chin (clear of the captions) or above the head, with a warning; pointers (`shorts.POINTERS`, e.g. sparkles, circle-highlight) stay where placed. With `auto_sound` (default on) a sparse sound pass is added at render, levelled against the measured voice, from real recordings: per family (whoosh, pop, click, camera, impact, riser, hit, success) the creator's pick on the Graphics tab (Sound effects: listen, then use; saved in `sfx-library/choices.json`, `GET/PUT /shorts/sounds`), else their own file in `sfx-library/<family>/`, else a default from the curated Mixkit catalog (`shorts.MIXKIT`; free for commercial videos, no credit; downloaded once to `models/sfx-mixkit/` and never redistributed as raw files), else Remotion's hosted sounds in `models/sfx-cache/`; impacts, risers and hits stay off until the creator picks or adds one. A job's `sfx/` copy follows a new pick at the next render; add `sfx` events only for extra moments. Back-to-back identical cards merge into one with a `timeline`; list rows appear when spoken. `flash` events draw the video's **switch effect** (`transition`: bloom default, leak, blur, whip, dip, flash, none; `motion/src/Transitions.tsx`), peaking at the event start where look switches begin; the creator picks it on the Graphics tab and re-planning keeps it, and its auto sound follows it (camera click for flash, soft whoosh for bloom/leak/blur/whip, none for dip). A `no_captions` event (`start`, `end`) hides the captions for words spoken in that window; add one only where the creator asks.
3. Plan graphics with `python shorts.py plan JOB` (prints the context: transcript, cuts, gestures, uploaded images). When the user asks Claude Code to plan, read that context and the keyframes, then write the plan JSON and save it with `--file`. `--auto` calls the Claude API (needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`; it sends the transcript, 12 keyframes and uploaded images to Anthropic). Save only valid plans. Report every warning, such as a hand sticker scheduled where no hand is tracked.
4. Render a draft (`python shorts.py render JOB`, half resolution), inspect it, then render the final (`--final`). A render works from a snapshot (`render-props.json`), so look, captions, graphics, sound and word fixes stay editable while it runs (`shorts_app.edit_locked`); building, planning and Ask Claude still wait. The live preview reloads when `plan.json`, `source.mp4` or `captions.json` change. Headless Chrome now and then captures a half-drawn frame (the picture repeated lower down, no captions); after each render `torn_frames` finds them and `repair_frames` renders each again as a still and re-encodes the video with them in place (sound copied unchanged). It also captures some frames before a layer under `filter: drop-shadow(…)` or `backdrop-filter` is redrawn while its content moves, so captions or glass cards blink out of the render though the live preview is fine: give captions depth with `text-shadow`, never a drop-shadow filter over moving letters; glass cards (`backdrop-filter`) still blink now and then (`torn_frames` catches the larger ones). Rendering is local. If headless Chrome crashes, set `SHORTS_BROWSER` to an installed Chrome.
   Post text: the Export tab's **Post it** (`POST /shorts/jobs/ID/post`, `shorts.post_copy`, one Claude call) writes 3 title options, the description (ending on a question) and 8-14 hashtags from the edit's own words, in Amharic with the English terms used, saved to `post.json` with copy buttons. When the user asks Claude Code for it, write `post.json` (`titles`, `caption`, `hashtags`, `best_time`) by hand instead of spending their key.
5. Never regenerate speech. Volume is levelled by default (`voice` in `workflow-mode.json`: `level` or `off`): at render, `voice-level.wav` is made from `source.mp4` with a slow speech rider (±8 dB), loudness normalisation to −14 LUFS and a peak limiter; gain only, `source.mp4` stays untouched, and sound effects are levelled against it. Apart from that, cutting is the only edit, except opt-in background-noise removal chosen per job (`noise` in `workflow-mode.json`): `light` = local highpass + RNNoise (model downloaded once to `models/`), `strong` = ElevenLabs Voice Isolator (cloud, paid; ask before choosing it for the user). The cleaned track is cached as `clean-<mode>.wav`, aligned to `upload.mp4`, used for transcription and the edit; `upload.mp4` stays untouched. Images the user uploads go in `runs/JOB/assets/` and are referenced by file name.
6. Screen recordings (B-roll) are uploaded next to images. `shorts.add_clip` converts each one to `assets/NAME.mp4` (H.264, 30 fps, muted), records it in `assets/clips.json` and saves 6 preview frames under `assets/.frames/NAME/`. A `broll` event (`asset`, `src_start`, `speed`, `layout` full|split|bubble|front, `corner`) shows it over the edit while the speaker's audio continues. Pick `src_start` from the preview frames so the screen matches the words. The plan check slows down or shortens clips that would run past the recording, skips overlaps, and falls back from `front` to `full` when no person cutout exists; report those warnings.
7. Hands-off is the default: the creator reviews, Claude edits. **Edit it for me** (upload checkbox, `POST /shorts/jobs/ID/auto`, or `python shorts.py auto JOB [--notes …]`) runs auto-cut → build + track → auto-plan (look + graphics) → draft render, as one cancellable step. It stops if `transcript-check.json` shows untranscribed speech instead of cutting it. Changes in words go through `POST /shorts/jobs/ID/revise` (`scope` graphics or edit); Claude receives the current plan and changes only what was asked. For single changes the editor has a **chat** on the left (`shorts_chat.py`, `GET/POST/DELETE /shorts/jobs/ID/chat`, `POST …/chat/undo`): Claude API tool calls edit one event, add a `no_captions` window, fix caption words (`edit_part`), change settings or the look, in place, without re-planning, rebuilding or rendering. It sees the timed transcript of the edit and the numbered events (text only), keeps the conversation in `chat.json`, and backs up the changed files per turn in `.chat-undo/` so each change can be undone. Each message spends the creator's `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` (`SHORTS_CHAT_MODEL`, default claude-opus-5-5). When the user asks Claude Code to edit a job, run `shorts.py auto` only after confirming it may spend their Anthropic key, or plan by hand with `--file`.
8. Background looks: the tracker cuts the person out with Robust Video Matting (ResNet-50 ONNX, ~107 MB in `models/`, 4 CPU threads, refined at full resolution with RVM's decontaminated edge colours and a slight choke; `SHORTS_MATTE=fast` uses the 15 MB MobileNet; MediaPipe is the fallback). `plan.background` places them on a look: `original`, `clean_blur`, `dark_glow`, `warm_studio`, `charcoal`, `cream`, `grid_paper`, `color_pop` and eight lit studio walls (`studio_blue`, `studio_coral`, `studio_teal`, `studio_violet`, `studio_rose`, `studio_forest`, `studio_slate`, `studio_sunset`) (`shorts.LOOKS`; no yellow), plus light (glow, rim, spotlight, vignette), subject (shadow, outline, scale) and **Pro finish**: `wrap` (match lighting: the wall colour wraps the cutout's edges and tints its shadows, an SVG filter), `reframe` (one steady chest-up punch-in from the median face, never exposing an edge; captions and LED tubes follow it), `tube` (lamp on the wall: side via `tube`, design via `lamp`: tube, twin, neon, globe, ring, arch, hex), `pattern` (light design on studio walls, `motion/src/wallLights.ts`: pool, halo, split, beam, wash, slash, blinds, window, leaves, bokeh, ring; anchored to the median face so the light sits behind the head, never floating above it; the planner may pick one, the creator picks on the Look tab and it is kept when they switch wall colour), `grade` (contrast + wall-coloured soft-light tone) and `grain`. Studio walls ship with Pro finish on. A `look` event (`start`, `end`, `look`) switches to another look for part of the edit (e.g. `original` to show the real room), cross-faded over 8 frames; a switch that ends where the next one starts stays solid under the next one's fade-in, so the real room never flashes between them; framing and grade stay. Add one only where the creator asks (Look tab: Switch look for a part); `plan_auto` drops switches Claude adds unless the creator’s own words ask for one, and the Look tab says when the preview stands inside a switch. The planner picks `look` unless the creator chose one (`background.auto` false). Rebuilding the edit keeps the look.
10. Client work: `clients.py` keeps clients in `clients.json` (name, colour, notes for Claude, brand kit; not in git). A video's `job.json` holds `client`, `delivery` ('' | sent | delivered, set by hand) and a copy of the client's kit taken at upload. `shorts.with_client` adds the client's notes to every Claude step (cut, plan, word check, post). A kit (`shorts.KIT_PLAN` + noise/voice/language) is saved from one video (`PUT /shorts/clients/ID/kit`), starts the client's new videos (`kit_plan`, all choices marked as the creator's so Claude keeps them) and can be applied to an existing one (`POST /shorts/jobs/ID/kit`, graphics kept).
9. The UI is a React + Vite app in `motion/studio/` (built to `static/studio/` by `make shorts` or `npm run studio:build`; dev server `npm run studio`). Pages: Videos (search, client and status filters), Clients (notes, brand kit, their videos), New video (client picker). The editor has a top bar (title, client, status, the one next action, ⋯ menu for brand kit and delete), section icons (Overview, Cut, Look, Captions, Graphics, Sound, Deliver), one shared live preview in the middle (sections pass unsaved changes through `ctx.preview.set`) and the section's settings on the right. It plays the edit live with the Remotion Player using the same `motion/src` components as the renderer (`GET /shorts/jobs/ID/props`, media under `/runs/ID/` via `MediaBase`). Themes are token sets in `motion/studio/src/theme.ts`.


## YouTube to Shorts

A front end to the Shorts editor at `/shorts?view=youtube` (UI, sidebar **From YouTube**) or `clipper.py` (CLI), routes in `clipper_app.py`. Only for videos the creator owns or may reuse. A link becomes a clip job in `runs/clips/ID/`; each moment made becomes an ordinary Shorts video in `runs/ID/` (`job.json` `source.kind` youtube), so every Shorts step and rule above applies to it.

1. `python clipper.py fetch URL [--language am|en|auto]` downloads the video with yt-dlp (video ≤1080p H.264 and audio separately, joined with PyAV into `source.mp4`: no ffmpeg; Node runs YouTube's player scripts), plus `info.json` (title, description, chapters, `heatmap` = YouTube's most-replayed graph). Transcript (`transcript.json`): the video's own captions in the spoken language (uploaded first, then automatic `-orig` ones; never one of YouTube's machine translations), free. Without captions: whisper.cpp for English; for Amharic it stops with `transcript-needed.json` until the creator approves ElevenLabs Scribe (paid per minute; `clipper.py transcribe JOB`, UI button). Those words are `exact` and are copied to each short as `seed-captions.json`, which `shorts.transcribe` uses once instead of a second paid transcript. `units.json` = `shorts.make_units` parts with a `replay` level (0-1). Videos over `CLIPS_MAX_MINUTES` (180) are refused.
2. Moments: `python clipper.py find JOB` prints the context (transcript parts, replay levels, chapters). When the user asks Claude Code to find them, read it and save `{"moments":[{"first":id,"last":id,"title","hook","kind","score","why"}],"summary":"…"}` with `--file` (times `start`/`end` in seconds also work). `--auto` is one Claude API call (`FIND_SYSTEM`; `CLIPS_MODEL`, default the planner model) with the transcript text only. A moment opens on its hook sentence and ends on the payoff, stands alone, one idea, no intros/sponsor reads; `hook` ≤ 8 words in the spoken language (Ethiopic for Amharic); `kind` viral|informative|key|story|funny|emotional|controversial; `score` 0-100. `save_moments` snaps to whole parts, pads (0.15 s in, 0.45 s out), drops moments under 8 s, over 180 s, backwards or overlapping a stronger one (with warnings: report them), ranks by score and keeps links to shorts already made. Lengths: auto 20-75 s, short 15-30, medium 30-60, long 60-90.
3. `python clipper.py make JOB [--moments 1,3] [--framing auto|follow|fit] [--track fast|full|off] [--edit]` cuts each moment (`cut_clip`: 30 fps, audio re-encoded once AAC 256k from a sample-counted slice) into a new Shorts video. Wide video becomes 1080×1920: `clipper_faces.py` (in `.venv-face`, MediaPipe on square tiles, 5 samples/s, shot changes on every frame) finds the speaker; per shot, a still speaker gets one steady crop at the median face, a moving one is followed smoothly, a sudden lasting face jump is a camera cut placed on the most-changed frame, and shots without a face are shown whole over a blurred fill (`fit`). Vertical video keeps its picture. Tracking defaults to `fast` (original background). With `--edit` (two Claude calls per short) `auto.json` carries `edit_notes`: keep the chosen moment whole (only dead air, false starts, filler go) and open on the hook as on-screen text. The UI queues shorts one at a time (`run_shorts` waits on each Shorts job), so the Mac never runs several trackers at once; **Make the shorts right away** (default) makes every moment found, otherwise the creator picks. Ask before running `--auto`/`--edit` on the user's key; downloading is free but large (about 1 GB per hour of 1080p).
4. If YouTube refuses a download, update yt-dlp (`pip install -U "yt-dlp[default]"`); report the real error.

## YouTube channel (faceless videos)

A faceless English YouTube channel from start to finish at `/shorts?view=channel` (UI: sidebar **YouTube channel**: Channel videos, Topic ideas, Niche research, Channels) or `channel.py` (CLI); routes in `channel_app.py`, helpers `channel_youtube.py` (YouTube Data API: research reads, OAuth upload), `channel_voice.py` (ElevenLabs), `channel_media.py` (fal.ai, Pexels/Pixabay), `channel_render.py` (shots, Faceless render). Claude Code writes everything (reports, scripts, shot plans, moments, posts) on the creator's plan with `graphics.run_claude` (API key removed, `dontAsk`, writes limited to the named files). Read `docs/channel-style.md` before writing for it. Install with `make channel`; check with `doctor.py --channel`. Paid steps (ElevenLabs credits, fal.ai, uploads) run only on the creator's click with the amount shown; the CLI asks first. YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule removes monetization from mass-produced videos: keep the creator's script review, cited sources and varied visuals.

1. Channel profiles live in `channels.json` (not in git; settings added later are filled from `DEFAULT_PROFILE` on read): audience, tone, notes, ElevenLabs voice (`voice.id`, `voice.model`), `look` (graphic style, backdrop, light/dark, two colours, music bed in `runs/channel/music/`, volume), `art` (`style` flat|doodle|3d|comic|realistic from `channel_media.ART_STYLES`, optional own `prompt`, `stock` on/off, `mascot` name, description, image in `runs/channel/mascots/<id>/mascot.png`), length (`minutes`, default 3: `channel.DEFAULT_MINUTES`), `ai_budget` (USD per video), upload defaults. New channels default to the cartoon look (flat illustrations, `cartoon` kit and backdrop, light). Mascot: `design_mascots` makes 4 options with fal.ai (paid, confirmed amount) or the creator uploads one; `pick_mascot` keeps it.
2. `python channel.py research "ideas" [--searches 2]`: `channel_youtube.scan` (free quota: search 100 units, lists 1; 10,000 a day; responses cached a day in `runs/channel/yt-cache`, units counted in `quota.json`) scores videos against their channel's median of recent long-form views and their subscribers, per seed: small (<50K subs) and young (<1 year) channel hits, top-3 share. Claude Code writes the report with `--json-schema` (`result.json`: summary, niches with verdict/demand/competition/faceless fit/RPM estimate/policy risk/evidence ids, topics based on outliers). Without `YOUTUBE_API_KEY` it is a labelled brainstorm.
2b. Topic research (what an existing channel makes next; sidebar **Topic ideas**, `python channel.py topics PROFILE [--focus …] [--competitors "@a,@b"] [--months 3]`): a research with `kind` topics in the same `runs/channel/research/` folders. `topic_scan` searches the channel's niche or the focus (at most 3 subjects, recent months only), reads the competitor channels' recent uploads scored against each channel's median (`yt.competitor_uploads`; competitors are saved on the profile), and collects what people type into YouTube search (`yt.suggestions`: Google's public autocomplete, free, best effort, cached a day). Claude Code (Read, WebSearch, WebFetch; `TOPIC_SCHEMA`; guide section 1b) returns 15–25 topics: evergreen or trending, hook, angle, 3 points, evidence (video ids, search phrases, links), never repeating the channel's own videos. **Make this video** passes the topic's angle, points, searches and sources as the video's notes (`topic_notes`) and records it in `made.json`.
3. Videos in `runs/channel/videos/ID/`. `script` (Claude Code researches with WebSearch/WebFetch, writes `sources.md` then `script.json`: sections hook|intro|chapter|rehook|outro with paragraphs, ~150 wpm, checked by `check-script`). Scripts are written as a performance (guide section 3): contractions, rhythm, punctuation as direction, CAPS on one stressed word, and ElevenLabs audio tags in square brackets (`[curious]`, `[whispers]`, `[short pause]`; about one every two to four sentences, never sound effects). `check-script` warns on overacting, sound-effect tags, round-bracket directions and a flat script; `perform` (UI: **Make it sound human**) has Claude Code add delivery to an existing script without changing its content. The creator reads, edits and **approves** (`job.json.approved` holds the script hash; any edit needs a new approval). The chat (`chat.json`, one Claude Code session) changes script, sources, shots or post; each change backs up to `.undo/` and `undo` steps back.
4. `voice`: one ElevenLabs `/with-timestamps` call per paragraph, cached in `voice/` by text+voice+model (+delivery unless natural). `channel_voice.speakable`: Eleven v4/v3 (`VOICE_MODELS[…]['tags']`; new channels default to `eleven_v4`) get the tags as written and no previous/next text; older models get `[pause]`-type tags as `<break time>` and other tags removed (they would be read aloud), plus previous/next text. Delivery (`voice.delivery` natural|expressive|steady) sets stability 0.5/0/1 on v3/v4, or style settings on older models (natural keeps the voice's own). Captions, word counts, shot planning and the post use `spoken()` (tags removed; `words_of` skips tag characters in the alignment). `voiced()` checks the voice-over against the voice it was made with (`words.json` voice/model/delivery and per-paragraph keys), so changing the channel's voice later never blocks a render; `voice_changed()` offers the re-voice. so an edit re-voices only that paragraph; joined with pauses, levelled to −14 LUFS → `voice.wav`; `words.json` has every word, paragraph and section time; `captions.srt` from it. The route refuses unless `credits` ≥ the current estimate. The free ElevenLabs plan's audio may not be used commercially.
5. `shots`: Claude Code plans `scenes.json` from the timed narration: each shot starts at a phrase of a paragraph (`p`, `at`), kind `image` (the main kind for illustrated looks; prompt describes only the scene, the art style is appended, `mascot: true` uses the mascot reference via the edit model), `ai_clip` (an illustration animated image-to-video, `animate`), `graphic` (a Films scene template with its own words in `props`), `chapter`, `stock` (realistic looks only). `check-scenes` times them into `shots.json` (shots 2.5–12 s, chapter cards ≤4.5 s, AI shots over the budget fall back to stock or words, mascot requests kept in the plan until a mascot exists).
6. `gather [--spend USD]`: stock is free; AI images/clips only within the approved spend and the budget left, each charge written to `costs.json` before the request; `visuals.json` records files and credits; swapping a stock clip uses its candidates. Models and prices are settings (`CHANNEL_FAL_*`); a changed prompt, style or mascot means new images (shown as a cost first).
7. `render [--final]`: the `Faceless` composition (`motion/src/faceless/Faceless.tsx`, 1920×1080): shots cross-fade over the voice, video and image shots move slowly, graphics reuse `film/SceneView`, chapter cards and a words fallback use the kit, music bed and sparse whoosh/pop from the Shorts sound library. The editor's preview plays the same composition (`GET …/props`).
8. Shorts: `moments` (Claude Code, `--json-schema`: runs of whole paragraphs, 15–75 s, hook ≤8 words; `moment_count`: 1–2 under 2½ min, 2–3 under 5 min, else 3–5); `shorts` renders each 9:16 from the same shots (`span`) and creates an ordinary Shorts job (`source.kind` channel, tracking off, voice off, `seed-captions.json` with the exact words, `auto.json` only with `--edit` and the Claude API key), then transcribes each one after another.
9. `package`: Claude Code writes titles, summary, tags, pinned comment, thumbnail texts (`post.json`); the description adds chapters (0:00 first, ≥3, each ≥10 s), sources from `sources.md`, stock credits and the AI-voice note; `thumbnails` renders three 1280×720 `Thumbnail` stills (`thumbs/1-3.jpg`). `finish` runs voice → shots → gather → draft → post after approval. `upload` (explicit click; `youtube-oauth.json` Desktop client + `youtube-token.json`, both out of git): resumable upload, private by default or scheduled (`publishAt`), `containsSyntheticMedia` only for realistic AI visuals, then thumbnail (needs a verified account) and captions. Uploads from an unaudited Google project stay private.

## Graphics library (Shorts)

Library graphics are `element` events (`element`: id, `props`: field values, plus `start`, `end`, `x`, `y`, `size`, `accent`). Each graphic is two files with the same name in `motion/src/elements/pack/` (shipped, MIT, part of the repo) or `motion/src/elements/mine/` (made on this machine; not in git):

- `name.json`: `id` (= file name), `name`, `category`, `description` (one line, what and when to use), `duration` (s), `layout` (`anchored`: drawn around x, y and scaled by size; `full`: whole frame), default `x`, `y`, optional `poster` (thumbnail moment 0-1), `sound` (whoosh, pop, click, impact, riser, success, none) and `fields` (`key`, `label`, `type`: text, longtext, color, number, select, toggle, list; `default`; `options`, `min`, `max`, `step`).
- `name.tsx`: `export default function X({ v, theme, kit, accent, durationInFrames, width, height, k }: ElementProps)`. Use `motion/src/elements/kit.tsx` (`useLife` enter/exit, `pop`, `ease`, `Starred` for *accent* words, `panel` for the video's graphic-style surface, `fit` to shrink long text, `str`/`num`/`list` to read fields). Designs are made for 1080 wide (multiply px by `k`), deterministic (`random(seed)` from remotion, no Math.random or timers), SVG ids from `useSvgId()` (never fixed ids: many graphics share one page), Ethiopic-ready (theme fonts), no yellow and no hard black text outlines. Text comes from fields, never hard-coded.

After adding or removing one: `cd motion && node scripts/elements.mjs` (registry), `npx tsc --noEmit`, then look at it: `node scripts/element-stills.mjs OUT_DIR ID` renders a still (at its poster moment; `--at 0.3` for another). Rebuild the studio (`npm run studio:build`) so it shows in the library. `shorts.element_catalog()` reads the JSON files; `check_plan` drops unknown graphics and cleans values to each field; the planner sees the library in its context and may place graphics where they fit the words (at most one at a time, never over the face).

Making and changing graphics (`library.py`, routes in `library_app.py`, the Graphics tab's "Describe a graphic" box): Claude Code headless (the creator's plan, never an API key) writes `elements/mine/ID.json` + `.tsx`, limited to those two files and `python library.py check ID` (registry, typecheck, review stills in `elements/mine/.review/`). A graphic that does not pass check is moved to `elements/mine/.history/ID/*-failed/` and never joins the library. Changes back up the old files first (`library.py undo ID`); pack graphics are never edited: a change makes `ID-mine`, and they can only be hidden (`elements/mine/hidden.json`), not deleted. One library task at a time; it runs only on the creator's click or request. When the user asks Claude Code directly in chat to make a graphic, write the two files by hand following this section and run `python library.py check ID`.

## Audio cleaner and transcriber

A separate local tool at `/cleaner` (UI) or `cleaner.py` (CLI): `python cleaner.py FILE [--noise light|strong|off] [--language am|en|auto] [--no-transcript]`. It takes any audio or video file, keeps an untouched copy as `input.*` in `runs/cleaner/JOB/`, and makes no generation calls. Noise removal reuses the shorts engines: `light` = local highpass + RNNoise, `strong` = ElevenLabs Voice Isolator (cloud, paid; ask before choosing it for the user). It writes `clean.wav` (aligned to the original, same length), `clean.mp3`, and for a video `clean-video.mp4` (picture packets copied unchanged, cleaned sound). Transcription runs on the cleaned sound when there is one: ElevenLabs Scribe with `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` (cloud, paid per minute, required for Amharic), else local whisper.cpp, which refuses Amharic with a clear error. Outputs `transcript.txt`, `transcript.srt` and `transcript.json` (timed words). Same language rule as shorts: do not use auto for Amharic. Afaan Oromoo (`om`, Oromifa) is best effort: Scribe does not list it, so the cleaner asks for `orm` and, if refused, transcribes without a language hint; whisper.cpp refuses it. Oromo is recognised from the transcript words (`looks_oromo`, no model download), also under auto; `transcript.json` keeps the service's own guess in `reported`. Tell the user the Oromo transcript is unverified quality. Meta MMS (local, about 6 GB with PyTorch) is the stronger option: ask before installing it.


## Motion graphics (HyperFrames)

A separate local workflow at `/graphics` (UI) or `graphics.py` (CLI) for promos, explainers and ads written as code (HTML + GSAP) and rendered to MP4 on this Mac by HyperFrames. It makes **no generation calls** and draws only text, shapes, charts and the creator's own images. Install once with `make graphics`; check with `doctor.py --graphics`. Read `docs/motion-graphics.md` before writing or changing a composition, and use the installed `/hyperframes` and `/motion-graphics` skills for framework rules.

1. `python graphics.py new TITLE [--size portrait|square|landscape] [--image FILE …] [--music FILE]` creates `runs/graphics/JOB/` with the starter composition, the local motion kit (`kit/motion-kit.js`), GSAP, fonts and placeholders. Images go in `assets/` and fill the next empty `imageN` slot; a transparent product image sets `productFit` to `cutout`.
2. Plan first, using the 4-part brief in `brief.json`: who/where/how long, the look (hex colors, fonts, mood), scene by scene with seconds, and rules. The creator writes it, or `graphics.py draft JOB "request"` (UI: **Draft the brief**) has Claude Code draft it from their words and images with `[placeholders]` for anything not given; they edit and save it. Ask for missing parts; do not invent brand colors, prices or claims. `python graphics.py prompt JOB` prints the build prompt. `graphics.py build JOB --model …` (UI: **Build with Claude Code**) runs Claude Code headless on the creator's plan (API key removed), limited to the project folder and the graphics.py check/snapshot/content/draft-render commands; it backs up `index.html` to `history/` (`undo` steps back one build or change at a time). `graphics.py change JOB "…"` (UI: **Ask Claude for a change**) makes one targeted design change with the same limits, reviewing only the moments it touched (about a minute). Building and changing spend plan usage: only on the creator's click or request.
3. Write `index.html` with every word, price, color and image path in the content block (`data-composition-variables`). Animate images with `MotionKit` recipes or plain GSAP at absolute times; deterministic only. On-screen text goes in the creator's language (Ethiopic script for Amharic).
4. `python graphics.py check JOB`, then `snapshot JOB --at …` and **look at the frames**. Fix cropped or overflowing text, contrast, empty frames, missing images or fonts, and elements that pop on or off. Lint alone is not approval. Then `render JOB` (draft) and inspect the draft.
5. Show the draft. Render `--final` after the creator approves it. Content edits (`content JOB --set id=value` or the Content form) need no new Claude run; re-render a draft after each.
6. Keep everything local: do not run `hyperframes publish`, `cloud render`, `lambda`, `cloudrun`, `auth`, `tts`, music generation or feedback/telemetry reports, and do not install large optional models (TTS, MusicGen/ACE-Step, background removal) without asking. Always use `graphics.py` or set `HYPERFRAMES_NO_TELEMETRY=1`; pass `--no-clipboard` to `hyperframes add`. Never regenerate or alter the creator's voice; music or voice-over is one `<audio id="music">` clip.
